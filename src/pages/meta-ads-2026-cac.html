How the math works · Assumptions

This model uses conservative industry benchmarks for DTC/e-commerce brands in the $1M–$25M revenue range. Every lever assumes a properly built, segmented email program — not just a basic welcome email. Actual results vary by category, AOV, and current flow maturity.

Welcome series lift: Assumes ~30% of monthly ad spend converts into new list subscribers, and a 5-point lift in welcome-series conversion rate. (adSpend × 0.30 / AOV) × 0.05 × AOV

Abandonment recovery: Assumes a multi-day consideration-phase flow captures an additional 3% of ad-driven traffic that currently drops off. This is the core "gap" Meta's March change created. (adSpend / CAC) × 0.03 × AOV

Post-purchase LTV: Assumes 8% of existing list (treated as approx. 60% existing customers) makes one additional purchase annualized from post-purchase flows, cross-sells, and win-back. Divided by 12 for monthly figure. (listSize × 0.60 × 0.08 × AOV) / 12

Paid-ad re-engagement: Assumes 15% of the list has been exposed to paid ads without converting, and a re-engagement flow closes 2% monthly. listSize × 0.15 × 0.02 × AOV × 0.5 (discounted for overlap with other levers).

Effective CAC: Treats monthly recovered email revenue as offset against ad spend, producing a blended CAC figure. (adSpend − monthlyRecovery) / (adSpend / CAC), floor at $1.

This is a modeling tool for directional planning, not a guarantee. The point is to show that a single-digit-percentage improvement across 4 common email levers produces a number worth paying attention to — especially against an attribution model that's actively working against your slow-burn converters.
